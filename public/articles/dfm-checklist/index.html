PCB DFM Checklist: A Practical Guide to Designing Boards That Build Right the First Time

A practical PCB DFM checklist: trace and space, drills, stackup, solder mask and panelization — the checks that keep a board from coming back for rework.

Published November 7, 2026

Get a free DFM review ↗Start an RFQ →

Design for manufacturability — DFM — is the habit of checking whether a board can actually be built before sending it to a fabricator. A design that passes its electrical rules check can still fail at the factory: a hole too small for the board thickness, a trace too close to the edge, a solder mask opening that does not match the pad. Each failure costs a rework, a respin, or a delivery delay. A DFM review is cheaper than any of them.

This checklist is organised the way a fabricator reads a design package: geometry first, then holes, then stackup and materials, then solder mask and silkscreen, then panelization, then the fabrication notes. Work through it in order and you will catch most problems before they reach the factory floor.

Trace and space: keep margin where you can

The most common DFM failure is a design that uses minimum geometry everywhere. Minimum trace width and clearance exist for the spots that genuinely need them — BGA fanout, dense connector areas — not for the whole board. Everywhere else, margin is free reliability. HXLFAB's minimum trace and space is 3/3 mil; treat that as the floor, not the default. Wide traces etch more reliably and carry more current, so keep high-current traces wide from the start and check current capacity against the copper weight on that layer.

Keep traces and copper pours back from the board edge — copper at the edge can be damaged during routing and can short when the board is depanelized. Leave clearance around mounting holes and slots too: a trace too close to a drilled hole can be nicked by the drill, and a mounting screw can short a pour that reaches the hole edge. Avoid acute angles in traces; a sharp inner angle can trap etchant and over-etch the copper, which is why 45-degree bends are the standard.

Holes: size, ratio, and position

A small hole in a thick board is hard to plate reliably because the plating chemistry has to reach the bottom of a deep, narrow barrel. That ratio — board thickness divided by hole diameter, the aspect ratio — is the number to check. HXLFAB builds boards from 0.15 to 7.0 mm finished thickness, with a 0.15 mm minimum mechanical drill and 0.10 mm laser-drilled microvias; a 0.15 mm hole in a 7 mm board is not a workable combination, so verify the ratio before ordering.

Do not mix drill sizes unnecessarily — each distinct size is a tool change, and a long drill table full of near-identical sizes adds cost and time without improving the board. State plated versus non-plated clearly in the drill table: a hole meant to be plated but drawn as non-plated, or the reverse, will be built wrong, and the mistake is often only found at assembly. Check the annular ring as well: the copper ring around each drilled hole must be wide enough to survive drill wander and registration tolerances. Finally, keep vias out of solder pads unless they are filled and capped — an open via in a pad wicks solder away from the joint and can leave a component unsoldered.

Stackup and materials: state them explicitly

Without a stackup drawing, the fabricator chooses defaults for layer order, dielectric thickness, and copper weight. Those defaults may produce a board that looks right and behaves wrong — especially for impedance-controlled designs. Include a stackup drawing in the fabrication notes: layer order, dielectric material and thickness, and copper weight per layer. HXLFAB builds from 1 to 30 layers, with 4 oz inner-layer and 8 oz outer-layer copper available, in FR-4 and PTFE materials with Tg135/150/170 grades — but the fabricator still needs to know which combination your design uses.

Specify impedance requirements per layer, with the target value, tolerance, and the nets they apply to; a single "50 ohm" note without a layer reference is not a specification. Match the material to the application: standard FR-4 suits most digital and low-frequency analogue designs, while PTFE is the choice for RF and high-frequency work, where dielectric loss and stability matter. State the Tg grade in the notes, and check that the finished thickness in the notes matches the stackup — a thickness that does not add up from the layer stack is one of the most common causes of a clarification email and a delay.

Solder mask and silkscreen

Solder mask and silkscreen look cosmetic, but they decide whether a board solders cleanly and whether anyone can assemble or debug it. Check solder mask openings against the pad sizes: an opening smaller than the pad leaves mask on copper that should solder, while one too large exposes copper that should be covered and risks bridging. Keep silkscreen off pads and out of solder mask openings — ink on a pad prevents soldering. Put reference designators where they can be read after assembly, not underneath components, and include polarity marks, pin-1 indicators, and connector orientation marks: cheap in the design, expensive to guess at assembly. State the solder mask colour in the fabrication notes, or the fabricator builds to its default.

Panelization and the board outline

Most boards are built in panels and separated later. Check the panel fit before ordering: HXLFAB's maximum panel size is 850 × 520 mm, and a board or panel arrangement larger than that cannot be built. Use routed edges with mouse bites (breakaway tabs) or V-scoring where appropriate, and keep components, traces, and copper clear of the break lines — a component sitting on a mouse-bite tab will be damaged when the panel is broken apart. Keep the board outline simple where possible; complex outlines with tight internal corners are harder to route and more expensive, and a radius on internal corners reduces stress cracking. Confirm the outline, mounting holes, and connector positions against the current mechanical drawing: mechanical fit is the easiest thing to get wrong and the easiest to check, and a board that does not fit its enclosure has to be remade.

Pre-fabrication checklist

  • [ ] Design-rule check clean, with minimum geometry (3/3 mil) used only where density requires it
  • [ ] Traces and copper pours clear of the board edge, mounting holes, and slots
  • [ ] Hole aspect ratio workable for the finished thickness (0.15–7.0 mm; 0.15 mm mechanical drill minimum, 0.10 mm laser microvia)
  • [ ] Drill table complete: sizes, plated vs non-plated, tolerances; annular ring adequate
  • [ ] No open vias in solder pads unless filled and capped
  • [ ] Stackup drawing included: layer order, dielectric thickness, copper weights (up to 4 oz inner / 8 oz outer)
  • [ ] Impedance requirements specified per layer with target value and tolerance
  • [ ] Material and Tg grade stated (FR-4 or PTFE; Tg135/150/170)
  • [ ] Solder mask openings match pad sizes; mask colour stated
  • [ ] Silkscreen clear of pads and mask openings; designators readable after assembly
  • [ ] Panel fit checked against the 850 × 520 mm maximum panel size; components clear of break lines
  • [ ] Outline, mounting holes, and connector positions match the current mechanical drawing
  • [ ] Gerber and drill files from the same revision; fabrication notes complete
